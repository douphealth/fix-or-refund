How long is the EU legal guarantee for faulty products?

Under Directive (EU) 2019/771, consumers buying from professional sellers in the EU get a minimum 2-year legal guarantee of conformity from delivery. Longer windows apply in Spain, Portugal, and Sweden (3 years), Ireland (up to 6 years to bring legal claims), and Finland & the Netherlands (expected lifespan with no fixed 2-year cap).

The store told me to contact the manufacturer—is that allowed?

No. Under EU law (ECC-Net), the seller who sold you the product is directly responsible for your statutory guarantee. A manufacturer's commercial warranty is optional and never replaces your rights against the store.

How does the 2026 Right to Repair +12-month warranty extension work?

Directive (EU) 2024/1799 (applicable from 31 July 2026 in transposed Member States) extends the statutory liability period once by +12 months whenever you choose repair over replacement for a defective product.

When can I demand a full refund instead of a repair or replacement?

You can escalate to a price reduction or full refund if repair or replacement is impossible or disproportionate, if the seller refuses or fails to complete it within a reasonable time without significant inconvenience, if the defect reappears after repair, or if the defect is serious enough to justify immediate termination.

Do I need the original box to claim my statutory guarantee?

No. You only need proof of purchase (such as a receipt, email invoice, or bank/card statement). Retailers cannot refuse a statutory guarantee claim because you no longer have the original packaging.
